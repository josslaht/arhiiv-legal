# Arhiiv Support

Arhiiv is a field log and light meter for film photographers. If something
isn't working, or you have a question this page doesn't answer, email
**[arhiivapp@gmail.com](mailto:arhiivapp@gmail.com)**. It helps to include the
version and build number from Arhiiv's Settings (the gear icon on the Shelf
tab).

## Your data

**Where is my data stored?**
Only on your iPhone. Arhiiv has no account and no server, and it doesn't sync
to iCloud yet. Your rolls, frames, gear and notes live in the app's own
storage on the device.

**What happens if I delete the app?**
Everything in it is deleted too. An iCloud or Finder backup of the whole phone
includes Arhiiv's data and restores it with the phone, but deleting and
reinstalling the app starts from empty.

**How do I keep a copy?**
Export your rolls as a CSV file:

- **Every roll at once:** Shelf → Film → the ••• menu → Export All Rolls.
- **One roll:** open the roll and use the export button under Actions.

The file carries each frame's aperture, shutter, lens, time, location and
note, plus the roll's film stock, format and camera body. Save it to Files,
iCloud Drive, or anywhere the share sheet reaches. Arhiiv can't import a CSV
back in yet, so treat it as a record rather than a restore.

## The light meter

**How accurate is it?**
Arhiiv reads the exposure your iPhone's camera has already worked out, and
converts it into aperture and shutter pairs for your film. It's a reflected
meter, like the one in most film cameras, not an incident meter. When a scene
is brighter or darker than the phone's camera can measure reliably, the meter
tells you. It also warns when a long exposure needs reciprocity correction.

**My readings are consistently off from my camera's meter.**
Set an offset in Settings → Meter calibration. It shifts every reading in
third-stop steps, so Arhiiv agrees with the meter you trust.

**The meter says it's paused.**
Another app, a phone call or Control Center took over the camera. The meter
resumes by itself when the camera is free again.

## Location

**Why does Arhiiv ask for my location?**
To record where each frame was shot. It takes a single fix when you log a
frame, only while the app is open, and stores it with that frame on your
phone. It's never sent to us. If you decline, frames log without a location
and everything else works the same.

## Known limitations

These are known, and on the list:

- **No iCloud backup or sync yet.** Data lives on one iPhone.
- **Exports can't be imported back.** CSV is one-way for now.
- **Push and pull are set through the ISO you load a roll at.** There's no
  separate field for box speed and shot-at speed yet.
- **35mm and 120 only.** Half-frame, large format and instant aren't modelled.
- **iPhone only**, and in English only.

## Coming next

In roughly this order: logging screens that match the meter, iCloud backup,
and then scans. That means bringing your lab scans into Arhiiv, matching them
to the frames you logged, and writing the exposure data into the image files
so it travels with them.

## Privacy

Arhiiv's [privacy policy](../) explains exactly what the app does with your
data. The short version: your log stays on your phone, and the anonymous usage
statistics can be turned off in Settings.
